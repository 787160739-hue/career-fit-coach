# Job-quality and scope-risk review

Read this reference for priority jobs, broad or ambiguous descriptions, title-to-duty mismatches, or roles whose quality materially affects application priority.

## Keep quality separate from fit

A user may be capable of doing a poor-quality job, and a good opportunity may be a credible stretch. Report job quality separately from capability and preference fit.

Classify observations as:

- **Positive signal:** explicit scope, decision rights, team context, measurable ownership, clear product or delivery stage, or transparent compensation.
- **Confirmed risk:** an explicit condition that conflicts with the user's constraints or materially changes the role.
- **Verify in interview:** broad ownership, “抗压,” rapid expansion, unclear bonus, customer-facing delivery, frequent travel, replacement hiring or ambiguous reporting lines.
- **Important unknown:** information absent from the posting that could change the decision.

## Review dimensions

- Scope concentration: one coherent role versus product + project + implementation + pre-sales without support.
- Title-duty alignment: whether the daily work matches the advertised title.
- Authority-accountability balance: whether responsibility is paired with decision rights and resources.
- Seniority-compensation alignment: compare requirements and package without treating missing salary as negative proof.
- Team and reporting context: manager, team size, collaborators, newly created versus replacement role.
- Work-pattern exposure: travel, customer escalation, on-call, overtime or cross-time-zone demands.
- AI authenticity: concrete model, data, knowledge, workflow, evaluation, deployment or operations work versus a renamed conventional system role.
- Posting freshness and duplication: source, inspection date and evidence that the role is still open.

Do not infer culture or work-life balance from industry, company size or slogans alone. Convert weak signals into concise recruiter questions.

## Output

Return:

1. positive signals;
2. confirmed risks;
3. verify-in-interview items;
4. important unknowns;
5. 3–5 recruiter questions;
6. how job quality changes application priority, if at all.
