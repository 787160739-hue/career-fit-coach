# Intake and profile workflow

Use progressive interviewing. Do not dump a long questionnaire when the user is already telling their story.

## Minimum decision set

Collect only what is missing:

1. Current role, industry, company context, dates and why the user is considering a move.
2. Recurring responsibilities and 2–4 representative projects.
3. For each project: problem, ownership, stakeholders, decisions, tools, delivery stage, result and evidence.
4. Tasks the user enjoys, tolerates and wants to avoid. Separate “good at” from “likes.”
5. Target cities or remote preference, industry openness, compensation floor, timing and work-life-balance expectations.
6. Education, credentials, language level and tools, distinguishing academic exposure from business use.

Ask one coherent cluster at a time. Reflect back a provisional interpretation and invite corrections before positioning.

## Project evidence prompts

When a statement is vague, ask for the smallest useful detail:

- What changed before versus after?
- How many users, vendors, records, workflows, systems or months were involved?
- What did the user personally decide or build?
- What was tested, accepted, launched or adopted?
- Is the number a validation rate, completion rate, cost saving, revenue outcome or operational proxy?
- What failed, and what did the user change?

Never pressure the user to invent numbers. Qualitative scope can still be strong evidence.

## Positioning output

Produce a compact profile with:

- one-sentence professional identity;
- evidence-backed capability clusters;
- preferred work pattern and avoid list;
- hard constraints and soft preferences;
- primary role family, adjacent role family and roles to avoid;
- search keyword matrix: role title × domain × capability × city.

Explain tradeoffs. For example, client-facing solution roles may fit skills while conflicting with a preference against frequent coordination or travel.
